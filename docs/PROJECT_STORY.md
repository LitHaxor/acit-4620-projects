## 1. The Problem and Why It Is Hard

Our goal is to estimate outdoor air pollution—specifically **PM2.5** (fine airborne particles with a diameter of 2.5 micrometres or less, about 30 times thinner than a human hair) expressed on the US Environmental Protection Agency **Air Quality Index (AQI)** scale—directly from a single daytime street-level photograph [L, CODE `src/data/cleaning.py`]. In machine learning (ML), predicting a continuous number like an AQI of `73.5` is called **regression** (predicting a continuous numeric quantity rather than a category, like predicting a house price of \$350,000 instead of just "cheap" or "expensive"), whereas predicting which of the six official US EPA pollution buckets a photo belongs to (`Good` `0–50`, `Moderate` `51–100`, `Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups` `101–150`, `Unhealthy` `151–200`, `Very Unhealthy` `201–300`, or `Hazardous` `>300`) is called **classification** (assigning an input to one of a fixed set of discrete buckets, like sorting incoming emails into "spam" or "inbox") [CODE `src/data/cleaning.py`, `src/metrics.py`].

Physically, the visual cue in a photograph comes from **Mie scattering** (the scattering of sunlight by airborne particles roughly the same size as the wavelength of visible light, which washes out sky colour and blurs distant building edges, much like car headlights glowing in fog) [C, CODE `src/data/transforms.py`]. As PM2.5 rises, distant objects lose contrast, the horizon turns milky white or grey, and colour saturation drops [CODE `src/features/haze_features.py`].

Why is this problem hard on our dataset? Most published camera-based AQI papers train and test on cameras from one city or a few fixed monitoring sites—where the background buildings, camera sensor, and local climate stay constant [C, UNVERIFIED]. By contrast, we use the crowdsourced global **PM25Vision** dataset (`DeadCardassian/PM25Vision`), which after cleaning contains `8,176` training images and `2,920` test images (`729` test monitoring stations, including `717` test images across `205` stations located more than `25 km` from any training station) [R2.a, R6.a, E, CODE `src/data/cleaning.py`]. In a global street-view dataset, two giant confounders fight the physical haze signal:

1. **Scene and weather diversity:** A grey overcast sky in a clean coastal city can look visually hazier than a sunny winter day in a polluted basin, and every street photo has different cameras, buildings, trees, and lighting [C].
2. **The geospatial shortcut:** In ML, a **shortcut** (or **spurious correlation**) is a pattern that lets a model get low error without learning the real task—like a medical model predicting disease by reading the hospital's name tag in the corner of an X-ray instead of looking at the lungs [C]. Because air pollution is heavily regional and seasonal, and many test stations sit in the same cities as training stations, a model given **metadata** (auxiliary non-image information attached to a sample, such as GPS coordinates and the month a photo was taken) can guess AQI surprisingly well just by memorising *where* and *when* the photo was taken, without looking at a single pixel [C, R2.a, R6.a].

### Paper 1: PFRML (Wang et al., *IEEE TGRS* 2026)

- **The problem it addresses:** Single-image AQI regression models struggle to combine fine local edge degradation with global sky-to-ground haze context, and pure regression ignores the discrete ordinal structure of the 6 AQI categories [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`, UNVERIFIED].
- **Main idea:** PFRML extracts four multi-scale **feature maps** (3D grids of numbers `[channels, height, width]` produced at successive layers of a visual network, where early maps have high resolution like `56x56` to see sharp edges and late maps have coarse resolution like `7x7` to summarise big regions) and fuses them in a **Dimension Consistency Module (DCM)** (`[PAPER PFRML Sec. II-B, Fig. 3, Table I]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/models/dcm.py]`). It passes the fused `14x14` tokens through a **Transformer encoder** (a neural network layer that uses **self-attention**—comparing every image patch against every other patch, like comparing a sky patch at the top of the photo with a building patch at the bottom—to capture global context) (`[PAPER PFRML Sec. II-B]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/models/dcm.py]`). Next, it learns $K=6$ class **prototypes** (one representative reference vector per AQI class stored in feature space, like having one "ideal average profile" for each of the 6 pollution levels) updated by an **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** (a running average that smoothly blends 90% of the old prototype with 10% of the new batch average, like a thermostat smoothing out sudden temperature spikes: $p_{\text{new}} = 0.9\,p_{\text{old}} + 0.1\,\bar{x}$) (`[PAPER PFRML Sec. II-C, Eq. 1–3]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/models/dcm.py]`). Finally, it trains with a **multitask loss** (a combined objective function that grades the network on two tasks at once: $L = L_{\text{cls}} + \beta L_{\text{reg}}$ with $\beta = 0.5$, like a course grade that counts 100% of your exam score plus 50% of your lab score) (`[PAPER PFRML Sec. II-D Eq. 5–6, Sec. III-C5]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/losses.py, src/pfrml_losses.py]`).
- **How our code uses it (and an important ambiguity in the paper):**
  - PFRML's own backbone follows a heavier ResNet-50 + Vision Transformer design trained with learning rate `2e-4`, batch size `16`, and a `1024 -> 256 -> 1` regression **Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)** (a stack of fully connected matrix-multiply layers separated by non-linear activations, like a two-step spreadsheet formula that transforms a list of numbers into a single output) (`[PAPER PFRML Sec. III-A3]`, `[C]`, `[CODE src/models/dcm.py]`). To stay lightweight on Apple Silicon (`mps`) and ~8k training images, our code substitutes **MobileNetV3-Small** (or `MDNet-T`) as the 4-stage backbone, projects all four stages to dimension $D=224$ at `14x14` spatial size in `DimensionConsistencyModule`, runs a 2-layer `LightTransformerEncoder`, and applies `PrototypeClassificationHead` (`K=6` L2-normalised prototypes on the unit hypersphere, squared Euclidean distance $\|x - p_k\|^2 = 2 - 2\cos\theta$, learnable temperature $\tau$, and `no_grad` EMA updates with decay `0.9` after a class-centroid initialisation at warmup epoch `3`) [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`, `train.py`].
  - **The Eq. 5 vs. Fig. 7 ambiguity:** In PFRML `Sec. II-D Eq. (5)` and `Fig. 5`, the regression MLP is written directly on the pooled backbone feature $\chi_i$: $\hat{y}_i = h(\chi_i)$ [C, CODE `src/models/dcm.py`]. However, in `Sec. III-C3 Fig. 7`, the ablation diagram explicitly labels the full model `"w/ prototype (prototype-weighted feature)"` versus `"w/o prototype"` [C, CODE `src/models/dcm.py`]. We implemented **both** in `PrototypeGuidedRegressionHead` (`src/models/dcm.py`):
    1. `reg_input="chi_proto"` (our R2 default): computes the prototype-weighted feature $\tilde{c}_i = \sum_{k=1}^6 p(y=k|\chi_i)\,c'_k$ and feeds the concatenated `448`-D vector $[\chi_i; \tilde{c}_i]$ into a `448 -> 256 -> 1` MLP [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`].
    2. `reg_input="chi"` (literal `Eq. 5` variant): feeds only the `224`-D vector $\chi_i$ (`224 -> 256 -> 1`) to the regression MLP while keeping the prototype classification loss $L_{\text{cls}}$ active in parallel [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`].
    3. `use_prototypes=False` (our R1 ablation, matching `Fig. 7 "w/o prototype"`): removes prototypes and $L_{\text{cls}}$ completely, training the DCM + Transformer + MLP (`224 -> 256 -> 1`) with regression-only Mean Squared Error (MSE) [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`, `src/pfrml_losses.py`].

### Paper 2: MDNet (Wang et al., *IEEE TII* 2024)

- **The problem it addresses:** Haze and particulate pollution affect visual features across multiple spatial scales at once—from tiny sharp building edges to wide patches of sky—so standard fixed-kernel **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)** (networks that scan small sliding pixel windows, such as `3x3` squares, across an image to detect edges and textures) have a limited **receptive field** (the size of the input pixel patch that influences a single output feature, like looking at a street through a narrow straw vs. a wide window) (`[PAPER MDNet Sec. I, Sec. III-A/B]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/models/mdnet.py]`).
- **Main idea:** MDNet introduces the **Multiscale Dilated Block (`MDBlock`)** (`[PAPER MDNet Sec. III-A/B, Fig. 1, Fig. 2]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/models/mdnet.py]`). Each `MDBlock` runs three parallel **depthwise dilated convolutions**—where **depthwise** means each filter operates on a single channel independently to save parameters, and **dilation** means inserting gaps of $d-1$ pixels between filter weights so a $k \times k$ filter covers an effective window of $k' = k + (k-1)(d-1)$ without adding extra weights (`[PAPER MDNet Eq. 3a–3c]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/models/mdnet.py`]). Specifically, the three parallel branches use `(k=3, d=2, p=2)` (effective `5x5` window), `(k=2, d=2, p=1)` (effective `3x3` window), and `(k=1, d=1, p=0)` (`1x1` window), sum their outputs, apply `GELU` activation, a `1x1` pointwise convolution, channel-wise `LayerNorm`, and a residual shortcut connection (`x + y`) (`[PAPER MDNet Sec. III-B, Fig. 2]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/models/mdnet.py]`). Stacking stage depths `[3, 3, 9, 3]` with channel widths `[96, 192, 384, 768]` forms `MDNet-T`, achieving a theoretical receptive field of `1136` pixels (`2288` for base `MDNet`) (`[PAPER MDNet Table III, Eq. 3a–3c]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[L]`, `[CODE src/models/mdnet.py]`). MDNet also uses label-smoothing cross-entropy ($\epsilon = 0.1$) for classification (`[PAPER MDNet Eq. 2]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/losses.py]`) and physics-preserving whole-image resize without colour jitter (`[PAPER MDNet Sec. III-C, IV-A]`, `[UNVERIFIED]`, `[CODE src/data/transforms.py]`).
- **How our code uses it (and the author's adaptation `E3b`):**
  1. **Direct implementation (`MDNet-T`):** In `src/models/mdnet.py`, `build_mdnet_t` implements the exact 4-stage `MDNet-T` architecture (`depths=(3, 3, 9, 3)`, `dims=(96, 192, 384, 768)`) trained from random initialisation ("from scratch", because no ImageNet weights exist for `MDNet-T`), supporting both continuous AQI regression and 6-class classification, as well as exposing `forward_stages` for PFRML's DCM [CODE `src/models/mdnet.py`].
  2. **Author's own adaptation (`MobileNetV3MDNeck`):** Because training a 4.9M-parameter backbone from scratch on `8,176` noisy global images is data-limited, we created an adaptation (`MobileNetV3-S + MD-Neck (Ref 2)`) that attaches two `MDBlock(128)` layers (after a `576 -> 128` `1x1` convolution and `LayerNorm2d`) onto an ImageNet-pretrained `MobileNetV3-Small` feature extractor [C, E, CODE `src/models/mdnet.py`]. In your report, you must state clearly that `MobileNetV3-S + MD-Neck (Ref 2)` is **our own adaptation** to test Paper 2's multiscale dilated block idea on top of pretrained features, not a model from Paper 2 [C, CODE `src/models/mdnet.py`].

## 3. The Data and the Rules That Keep Evaluation Honest

If you evaluate an ML model carelessly on geospatial photo data, you can easily fool yourself into thinking a memorisation engine is a great computer-vision model [C]. Here are the six data and evaluation rules we enforced across the codebase and *why* each one exists:

1. **Deterministic Data Cleaning (Purging Train/Test Overlap & Multi-Station Deduplication):**
   - *What we did:* `src/data/cleaning.py` removes `18` `image_id`s from the training split that also appeared in the test split, and deduplicates images associated with multiple nearby stations by taking the **median** AQI (`pm25`) (the middle value when numbers are sorted, so extreme outliers among nearby sensors do not skew the label—e.g., the median of `[40, 42, 190]` is `42`) and recomputing the 6-class US EPA bin [L, CODE `src/data/cleaning.py`]. This produces `8,176` cleaned training images and `2,920` cleaned test images [R2.a, CODE `src/data/cleaning.py`].
   - *Why it exists:* If the exact same photograph sits in both train and test, or appears multiple times in the same split with conflicting station rows, validation and test scores are artificially inflated by **data leakage** (when information from the evaluation set sneaks into training, like a student seeing the exact exam questions the night before) [C, CODE `src/data/cleaning.py`].

2. **Station-Grouped Cross-Validation (`station` mode) — Never Random Splits:**
   - *What we did:* Instead of a single train/validation split, we use 5-fold **cross-validation (CV)** (splitting the training data into 5 equal parts, training on 4 parts and validating on the 5th part 5 separate times so every image is validated once). Specifically, we use `StratifiedGroupKFold` (`src/data/splits.py`), which enforces **stratification** (keeping the proportion of the 6 AQI pollution bins balanced across all 5 folds) while grouping by `station_id` so all photos from a given monitoring station stay in the same fold (zero station overlap between train and validation) [L, CODE `src/data/splits.py`].
   - *Why it exists:* Many photos come from the same fixed camera or street corner [C]. Under a random image split, a CNN can recognise the buildings and trees of a specific station seen in training and copy its usual AQI instead of reading the atmospheric haze [C, CODE `docs/AGENT_RULES.md`].

3. **25 km Spatial-Cluster Cross-Validation (`spatial_cluster` mode) for Multimodal Fusion:**
   - *What we did:* In `src/data/splits.py`, we compute pairwise **Haversine distances** (great-circle distances in kilometres over the curved surface of the Earth between GPS latitude/longitude pairs) and group stations using **single-linkage agglomerative clustering** at a `25.0 km` threshold (a "friends-of-friends" grouping algorithm: if Station A is within `25 km` of Station B, and Station B is within `25 km` of Station C, all three are linked into the same city cluster) [C, L, CODE `src/data/splits.py`]. All stations in a cluster are forced into the same CV fold [CODE `src/data/splits.py`].
   - *Why it exists:* Even when `station_id` does not overlap, a big city has many distinct stations only a few kilometres apart [C, L]. When a model takes GPS coordinates as an input (in multimodal fusion), station-grouped CV still lets the model interpolate pollution from a neighbouring station `2 km` away [C, L]. Spatial-cluster CV guarantees that every validation station is at least `~25 km` away from the nearest training station, testing whether fusion works in unseen cities/regions where location alone is least helpful [C, L, CODE `src/data/splits.py`].

4. **Physics-Preserving Augmentations (Strictly No Colour Jitter or Cropping):**
   - *What we did:* In `src/data/transforms.py`, images are resized directly to `224x224` without cropping (preserving both the upper sky region and lower ground/building region), normalised with ImageNet mean/standard deviation, and augmented during training *only* with random horizontal flips ($p=0.5$) and gentle affine transforms ($\pm 5^\circ$ rotation, $\pm 2\%$ translation and scaling) [L, CODE `src/data/transforms.py`]. Hue, saturation, brightness, contrast jitter, and grayscale conversion are completely banned [C, CODE `docs/AGENT_RULES.md`, `src/data/transforms.py`].
   - *Why it exists:* In standard object recognition (like classifying dogs vs. cats), randomly changing brightness or colour saturation helps the model ignore lighting; in AQI estimation, brightness, contrast, and colour saturation **are** the physical Mie scattering signal [C, CODE `docs/AGENT_RULES.md`, `src/data/transforms.py`]. Random colour jitter would randomly scramble the ground-truth AQI cue relative to its label [C].

5. **Hyperparameters Chosen on Validation Folds Only; One-Shot Test Evaluation:**
   - *What we did:* All **hyperparameters** (settings chosen before training, like learning rate or number of epochs, as opposed to weights learned during training) and architecture choices were selected using CV on the `8,176` training images [C]. For Stage 2 of the image-backbone queue, a deterministic rule was fixed in advance: keep the default (`lr=1e-3`, `30` epochs) unless a candidate improves Fold-0 validation MAE by at least `1.0` AQI point [C, CODE `scripts/experiments/queue_baselines.py`]. Only one script in the repository (`scripts/final_evaluation.py`) evaluates neural models on the test set, and it checks `final_test_results.csv` first—refusing with exit code `1` if a model `--name` has already been evaluated [C, CODE `scripts/final_evaluation.py`].
   - *Why it exists:* Checking the test set repeatedly while tweaking models turns the test set into a validation set, causing overfitting to the test split [C]. The one-shot guard guarantees that test numbers are a clean, uncheated check [C, CODE `scripts/final_evaluation.py`].

6. **Last-Epoch Reporting (Why Best-Epoch Selection Was Abandoned):**
   - *What we did:* Every neural network training run logs a full per-epoch curve (`results/curves/*.csv`), and all authoritative CV and test tables in `results/REPORT_NUMBERS.md` (Sections 3–6) report the **last epoch** of the fixed cosine learning-rate schedule rather than the epoch with the lowest validation loss (`best_ep`) [C, R3, R4, R5, R8].
   - *Why it exists:* Early runs saved the checkpoint with the minimum validation loss (`val_loss`) [C, R8]. That caused two serious distortions:
     1. In PFRML multitask models, `val_loss` is the sum of classification **cross-entropy** (a loss function that heavily penalises confident wrong class predictions, like losing 10 points if you say you are 99% sure of the wrong answer) plus $0.5 \times \text{MSE}$ [C, CODE `src/losses.py`]. As training progressed past epoch 6, the 6-class classifier became over-confident on unseen stations, making `val_loss` rise even while regression MAE kept improving [C, CODE `scripts/analysis/diagnose_training_failures.py`]. Picking `min(val_loss)` prematurely froze PFRML models around epochs `1–6` of `30` [C].
     2. On single-task regression, picking `min(val_loss)` on noisy Fold 3 selected epoch 1 for `ResNet-18 Pretrained Tuned`, artificially inflating its best-epoch CV mean compared to its converged last-epoch CV mean (`44.05 +- 8.70` last-epoch MAE) [C, R3.a, R8].
     3. Even more fundamentally, picking the best epoch per fold on the validation set is a mild form of validation peeking that cannot be replicated when training a single final model on 100% of the training data without a validation split [C, CODE `scripts/final_evaluation.py`].

### Group 1: Non-Deep and Metadata Baselines (`Constant Median Predictor`, `Haze-Statistics Ridge Regression`, `GPS + Month Metadata MLP`)

- **The question:** What is the trivial error floor if we predict a constant number, how much signal is in simple handcrafted haze formulas without deep learning, and how strong is the non-visual GPS + month shortcut on its own [E, C]?
- **The setup:** We evaluated three fast baselines across 5-fold `station` CV (`n=8,176`), 5-fold `spatial_cluster` CV (`n=8,176`), the full test set (`n=2,920`), and the `>25 km` test subset (`n=717`) [R2.a]: (1) the **Constant Median Predictor**, which always outputs the median AQI of the training split; (2) **Haze-Statistics Ridge Regression**, which extracts `18` deterministic atmospheric statistics (mean and standard deviation of HSV colour saturation, Sobel edge-gradient contrast, and the **Dark Channel Prior**—the minimum RGB value in a `15x15` pixel patch, which increases as haze brightens dark shadows—across the `full`, `top`, and `bottom` image halves) and fits a **Ridge regression** model (a linear regression model with an $L_2$ penalty $\alpha \sum w_i^2$ that shrinks weights to prevent overfitting, with $\alpha \in \{0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1000\}$ chosen strictly on the training fold via `RidgeCV`); and (3) the **GPS + Month Metadata MLP** (`5 -> 64 -> 32 -> 1`, trained for `40` epochs with `AdamW`), which reads only the 3D unit-sphere GPS coordinates $(x, y, z) = (\cos\text{lat}\cos\text{lon}, \cos\text{lat}\sin\text{lon}, \sin\text{lat})$ and cyclic month $(\sin(2\pi m/12), \cos(2\pi m/12))$ without touching the image pixels [E, CODE `src/data/dataset.py`, `src/features/haze_features.py`, `src/models/baselines.py`, `train.py`].
- **The result:**
  - **Constant Median Predictor:** `station` CV **Mean Absolute Error (MAE)** (the average absolute difference $\frac{1}{n}\sum |y_i - \hat{y}_i|$ between true and predicted AQI, so an MAE of `66` means predictions are off by 66 AQI points on average) = `66.21 +- 4.89` (**Coefficient of Determination $R^2$** = `-0.0161 +- 0.0123`, where $R^2$ measures the fraction of variance explained relative to the mean: `1.0` is perfect, `0.0` is no better than guessing the mean, and negative is worse than the mean); `spatial_cluster` CV MAE = `66.14 +- 5.60` ($R^2 = -0.0168 +- 0.0142$); Full test MAE = `63.64` ($R^2 = -0.0105$); Test `>25 km` MAE = `56.60` ($R^2 = -0.0049$) [R2.a].Notice also that per-fold validation MAE for the `Constant Median Predictor` in `station` mode is `F0=67.18, F1=62.31, F2=63.71, F3=74.33, F4=63.50`, proving that Fold 3 is intrinsically the hardest fold (`74.33` MAE) because a handful of extreme stations (Delhi/NCR, Saltillo/Monterrey, Chiang Rai, Pune) concentrate most of Fold 3's `AQI > 200` tail [R2.b, C, L].
  - **Haze-Statistics Ridge Regression:** `station` CV MAE = `64.11 +- 4.40` (**Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient (PLCC)** = `0.1998 +- 0.1083`, measuring linear correlation from `-1` to `+1`; **Spearman Rank Correlation Coefficient (SRCC)** = `0.2354 +- 0.0695`, measuring rank-order correlation; $R^2 = 0.0301 +- 0.0583$); `spatial_cluster` CV MAE = `64.56 +- 5.02` ($R^2 = -0.0018 +- 0.0503$); Full test MAE = `60.84` ($R^2 = 0.0408$); Test `>25 km` MAE = `53.76` ($R^2 = -0.0377$) [R2.a].
  - **GPS + Month Metadata MLP:** `station` CV MAE = `43.15 +- 7.47` (**Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)** = `65.43 +- 18.12`, which squares errors before averaging and taking the square root $\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}$, punishing large outlier mistakes more than MAE; PLCC = `0.6704 +- 0.1236`; SRCC = `0.7260 +- 0.0762`; $R^2 = 0.4438 +- 0.1626$; 6-class accuracy `Acc` = `0.4234 +- 0.0329`; adjacent-class accuracy `+-1Acc` = `0.8794 +- 0.0360`, the fraction of predictions within at most 1 AQI bin of the true bin; **Macro-F1** = `0.3506 +- 0.0289`, the unweighted average across all 6 classes of the harmonic mean of precision and recall, where precision/recall both being `0.50` gives $F_1 = 0.50$); `spatial_cluster` CV MAE = `46.00 +- 7.33` ($R^2 = 0.3647 +- 0.1768$); Full test MAE = `40.34 [34.45, 48.31]` ($R^2 = 0.4982$, `Acc = 0.4096`); Test `>25 km` MAE jumps to `49.84 [40.18, 58.81]` while $R^2$ collapses to `-0.0393`, `Acc` drops to `0.2929`, and `TailF1` (Macro-F1 on bins 5–6, `AQI > 200`) hits `0.0000` [R2.a, R6.a].
- **What it means in one plain sentence:** Simple handcrafted haze numbers barely beat guessing the median (`64.11` vs `66.21` MAE), while GPS + month alone gets a deceptively strong `40.34` MAE on the full test set by memorising nearby training cities before collapsing completely ($R^2 = -0.0393$, worse than guessing the average) on stations more than `25 km` away [R2.a, R6.a, C].

## 5. What Worked, What Did Not, and What Failed to Train

In your report, it is crucial to separate **ESTABLISHED** empirical findings (backed by 5-fold paired comparisons or 95% station-bootstrap confidence intervals) from **HYPOTHESIS** explanations (plausible mechanisms that were not directly tested) [C]:

### What Worked
- **ESTABLISHED — ImageNet pretraining is the largest visual accuracy lever:** Across 5-fold `station` CV, all three pretrained vision models (`ResNet-18 Pretrained Tuned` at `44.05 +- 8.70`, `MobileNetV3-S + MD-Neck (Ref 2)` at `44.92 +- 8.54`, and `MobileNetV3-S Pretrained (Ref 1)` at `45.66 +- 8.41`) beat both from-scratch models (`ResNet-18 Scratch (30 ep)` at `53.27 +- 7.11` and `MDNet-T Regression Scratch (60 ep)` at `53.29 +- 6.38`) by `~8–9` MAE points on every single fold [R3.a, C].
- **ESTABLISHED — Multimodal fusion (R1 visual neck + Metadata MLP, early-stopped at 7 epochs) beats both single modalities on the full test set:** `Final Winning Fusion Model (ep=7)` (`35.77` MAE, $R^2 = 0.6070$) outperforms `GPS + Month Metadata MLP` (`40.34` MAE, paired diff `-4.57 [-8.31, -1.26]`) and `Final Image-Only Baseline` (`41.12` MAE, paired diff `-5.35 [-8.62, -2.26]`), with 95% station-bootstrap CIs excluding zero [R6.a, R6.b, C].
- **ESTABLISHED — Far from training stations (`>25 km`), visual models generalise while metadata-only collapses:** On `test_gt25km` (`n=717`, `205` stations), `GPS + Month Metadata MLP` collapses to `49.84` MAE and $R^2 = -0.0393$, whereas `Final Winning Fusion Model (ep=7)` (`39.22` MAE, $R^2 = 0.3743$, paired diff `-10.63 [-18.78, -2.59]` vs metadata-only) and `Final Image-Only Baseline` (`39.60` MAE, $R^2 = 0.2747$, paired diff `-10.24 [-20.50, +0.86]` vs metadata-only) retain positive skill [R6.a, R6.c, C].

### What Did Not Help
- **ESTABLISHED — PFRML's prototype + multitask head hurt regression accuracy compared to a plain regression head:** In 5-fold `station` CV, removing prototypes (`R1: DCM + Transformer (No Prototypes)`, `45.50 +- 8.64`) beat the prototype baseline (`R2: PFRML Baseline (Toggles Off)`, `48.14 +- 7.61`) by `-2.63 +- 1.10` MAE (`4/5` folds won) and beat the literal Eq. 5 variant (`R2 (Eq. 5 Literal: reg_input=chi)`, `48.04 +- 8.20`) by `-2.54 +- 0.42` MAE (`5/5` folds won) [R4.a, C]. On the test set, `Final Image-Only Baseline` significantly beat `Final Full PFRML Vision Model` both overall (`41.12` vs `45.04`, paired diff `-3.92 [-6.27, -1.58]`) and at `>25 km` (`39.60` vs `46.79`, paired diff `-7.18 [-11.92, -2.53]`) [R6.a, R6.b, R6.c, C].
  - **HYPOTHESIS (why the prototype head cost accuracy):** Because 6-class accuracy is only `~0.40–0.41` on unseen global stations, the joint cross-entropy loss $L_{\text{cls}}$ competes with regression for shared representation capacity, becomes over-confident on training stations, and injects noisy 6-class prototype probabilities into $[\chi_i; \tilde{c}_i]$ [C, L].
- **ESTABLISHED — Literature toolbox toggles (`T1` attention pooling, `T2` physics gate, `T3` class weights) gave at most `~1` MAE improvement over `R2`, within cross-fold noise:** `R2 + T3: Class Weights` (`-1.08 +- 0.49` vs `R2`), `R2 + All Approved Toggles (T1+T2+T3)` (`-0.98 +- 0.90` vs `R2`), and `R2 + T2: Physics Prior Gate` (`-0.57 +- 0.86` vs `R2`) improved slightly over `R2` on `4/5` folds, while `R2 + T1: Attention Pooling` was `+0.27 +- 0.32` worse, and none recovered the accuracy of `R1` (`45.50`) or `MobileNetV3-S Pretrained (Ref 1)` (`45.66`) [R4.a, C].
- **ESTABLISHED — Paper 2's `MDNet-T Regression Scratch (60 ep)` did not beat `ResNet-18 Scratch (30 ep)`:** The two from-scratch models tied in 5-fold `station` CV (`53.29 +- 6.38` vs `53.27 +- 7.11` MAE) [R3.a, C].
  - **HYPOTHESIS (why `MDNet-T` from scratch did not beat `ResNet-18` from scratch):** Possible reasons—such as `8,176` heterogeneous street scenes being too small for `MDNet-T`'s 4.9M parameters without pretraining, or suboptimal regularisation—were not tested [C].
- **ESTABLISHED — Modality dropout (`p_drop=0.3`) and the auxiliary vision head did not stop the fusion regression head from relying on metadata:** In 5-fold `spatial_cluster` CV, zeroing metadata at evaluation time degraded `Fusion R1 (p_drop=0.3, 30 ep)` from `49.18` to `52.28` MAE and degraded `Final Winning Fusion Model (ep=7)` on the full test set from `35.77` to `47.56` MAE (`Final Fusion (Metadata Zeroed at Eval)`), which is significantly worse than `Final Image-Only Baseline` (`41.12` MAE, paired diff `-6.44 [-9.39, -3.22]`) [R5.a, R6.a, R6.b, C].
  - **HYPOTHESIS (why fusion overfits and relies on metadata):** The low-dimensional `MetadataMLP` branch memorises training station locations much faster than the CNN/Transformer extracts generalisable haze cues, so the shared regression MLP assigns large weights to the `32`-D metadata coordinates $m$ and shifts its bias when $m$ is zeroed out [C, L].

### What Failed to Train (Optimization Failures)
- **ESTABLISHED — `Extra B: MDNet-T PFRML` (`pfrml_mdnet_t` from scratch at `lr=1e-3`, `60` ep) collapsed completely across all 5 folds:** Mean `train_loss` did not decrease from epoch 1 (`2.1609`) to epoch 60 (`2.1784`), last-epoch $R^2$ stayed at `0.0054 +- 0.0151` (`|R2| < 0.05` on `5/5` folds), and last-epoch MAE (`66.56 +- 4.88`) matched the `Constant Median Predictor` (`66.21 +- 4.89`) [R4.a, R7, CODE `scripts/analysis/diagnose_training_failures.py`]. Lowering the learning rate to Paper 1's `lr=2e-4` on Fold 0 (`MDNet-T PFRML Lower-LR Probe`, `58.15` MAE, `train_loss` `2.2084 -> 1.9243`) avoided total collapse but remained `9.78` MAE points worse than regression-only `MDNet-T Regression Scratch (60 ep)` on Fold 0 (`48.37`) [R3.a, R7, C]. This is an optimisation failure in our from-scratch setting, **not** proof that PFRML + MDNet cannot work in general [C].
  - **HYPOTHESIS (why `pfrml_mdnet_t` from scratch failed to train):** Jointly optimising a 4-stage dilated backbone, a 2-layer Transformer, and an EMA prototype head from random initialisation on `~6.5k` per-fold images likely requires a lower learning rate (`<= 2e-4`), longer warmup, or staged pretraining [C].
- **ESTABLISHED — `R2 + T4: DCM Concat` failed to converge across all 5 folds:** Replacing element-wise summation in the DCM with `4 x 224 -> 224` concatenation + `1x1` conv + `LayerNorm2d` caused `train_loss` to stall (`2.0567` at epoch 1 to `1.9196` at epoch 30, failing the `< 0.5x` learning criterion on `5/5` folds), yielding `60.51 +- 5.41` MAE and $R^2 = 0.0556 +- 0.1127$ [R4.a, R7, CODE `scripts/analysis/diagnose_training_failures.py`].

## 6. The Headline Findings in 5–6 Sentences

On the global crowdsourced `PM25Vision` benchmark (`8,176` training images, `2,920` test images across `729` stations), a non-visual `GPS + Month Metadata MLP` achieves `40.34` test MAE by exploiting spatial proximity to training stations, but collapses completely ($R^2 = -0.0393$, `49.84` MAE) on the `717` test images located more than `25 km` from any training station [R2.a, R6.a, C]. Among pure computer-vision models, ImageNet pretraining is the dominant factor—reducing 5-fold `station` CV MAE by `~8–9` points (`44.05–45.66` vs `53.27–53.29` for from-scratch `ResNet-18` and `MDNet-T`)—while our lightweight `MobileNetV3-S + MD-Neck (Ref 2)` adaptation (`44.92 +- 8.54` MAE) and plain `MobileNetV3-S Pretrained (Ref 1)` (`45.66 +- 8.41` MAE) statistically tie `ResNet-18 Pretrained Tuned` (`44.05 +- 8.70` MAE) at a fraction of the parameter count [R3.a, C]. ## 7. Limitations and Threats to Validity

- **Single random seed (`seed=42`):** Every training run used a single fixed seed (`42`), so run-to-run optimisation noise is not averaged out and MAE differences smaller than roughly `1–2` AQI points cannot be separated from random training variance [C, R1].
- **Five CV folds cap non-parametric statistical power:** With $n=5$ folds, a two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test has a minimum possible $p$-value of $0.0625$, meaning CV comparisons must rely on paired mean differences $\pm$ SE and folds won (`/5`) rather than $p < 0.05$ significance [C, L, R4].
- **Fold-0 hyperparameter screening optimism:** Stage 2 of the image-model queue selected learning rates and epoch counts (`ResNet-18 Pretrained Tuned`, `MobileNetV3-S + MD-Neck (Ref 2)`, `MDNet-T Regression Scratch (60 ep)`) on Fold 0, which is also included in the 5-fold CV average—making their 5-fold CV means slightly optimistic relative to untuned models [C, CODE `scripts/experiments/queue_baselines.py`].
- **Same-folds epoch selection for `Fusion R1 (p_drop=0.0, CMA-3 ep=7)`:** The early-stopping epoch count $N=7$ was chosen from the 5-fold validation curves of `Fusion R1 (p_drop=0.0, 30 ep)` and re-validated on those same 5 folds (`44.40 +- 7.30` MAE), so `44.40` is slightly optimistic and the one-shot test evaluation (`35.77` MAE) serves as the clean check [C, R5.a, R6.a].
- **Small `>25 km` test subset (`n=717`, `205` stations) and empty extreme tail:** The `test_gt25km` subset has wide station-bootstrap confidence intervals (e.g., `[-20.50, +0.86]` for `Final Image-Only Baseline` vs `GPS + Month Metadata MLP`) and every model records `TailF1 = 0.0000` there, so `>25 km` tail classification metrics should be ignored [C, R6.a, R6.c].
- **Unresolved disagreement between `spatial_cluster` CV and `test_gt25km` on image vs. location:** In 5-fold `spatial_cluster` CV, `GPS + Month Metadata MLP` beat `Extra A: E2 Spatial-Cluster Reference` on `5/5` folds (`46.00` vs `50.69` MAE), whereas on `test_gt25km` `Final Image-Only Baseline` had a lower point MAE than `GPS + Month Metadata MLP` (`39.60` vs `49.84`, CI `[-20.50, +0.86]`) (**HYPOTHESIS:** the `205` `>25 km` test stations may have a different geographic/climatic distribution or lower extreme-tail share—`Constant Median Predictor` MAE is `56.60` on `test_gt25km` vs `66.14` in `spatial_cluster` CV) [C, R2.a, R5.a, R6.a].
- **Backbone and hyperparameter substitutions relative to the papers:** Rather than PFRML's heavier ResNet-50 + ViT architecture (`lr=2e-4`, batch size `16`, `1024 -> 256 -> 1` MLP), our main PFRML ablation ladder used `MobileNetV3-Small` (`lr=1e-3`, batch size `32`, `D=224`, 2-layer Transformer, `448/224 -> 256 -> 1` MLP) to fit laptop hardware and dataset size [C, CODE `src/models/dcm.py`, `configs/default.yaml`].
- **PFRML paper ambiguity (`Eq. 5` vs. `Fig. 7`):** PFRML's text/equation (`Sec. II-D Eq. 5`, $\hat{y}_i = h(\chi_i)$) and ablation figure (`Sec. III-C3 Fig. 7`, `"prototype-weighted feature"`) describe two different regression-head inputs, though testing both (`48.04` vs `48.14` MAE) showed the ambiguity did not change the outcome [C, R4.a, CODE `src/models/dcm.py`].
- **Extreme spatial concentration of hazardous pollution (`Fold 3` skew):** Because 80% (`282/355`) of training `Bin 5` (`AQI > 300`) images sit in just three city clusters (Beijing, Delhi/NCR, Saltillo/Monterrey), grouping stations by location inevitably makes Fold 3 much harder for every model (`Constant Median Predictor` MAE `74.33` on Fold 3 vs `62.31–67.18` on other folds), inflating cross-fold standard deviations [C, L, R2.b].
- **Crowdsourced label and visual noise:** Global street-level photos vary in camera quality, exposure, viewing angle (sky share), and distance/time offset from the reference monitoring station, and potential label noise was not audited visually [C].
- **Apple MPS GPU floating-point non-determinism:** PyTorch on the Apple Silicon `mps` backend is not bit-exact across runs, so retraining from scratch reproduces metrics approximately (within normal stochastic optimisation variance) rather than to the last decimal place, whereas evaluating saved checkpoints/predictions is exact [C, L].

## 11. Questions an Examiner May Ask (With Short Model Answers)

1. **Q: Why did you group cross-validation splits by `station_id` and `25 km` spatial clusters instead of using a standard random 80/20 split?**
   - **A:** In `PM25Vision`, many images come from the same fixed camera station, and stations are tightly clustered inside major cities [C, L]. A random split would let a CNN memorise background buildings (`station_id`) and let a metadata model interpolate AQI from a station a few kilometres away; grouping by `station_id` eliminates camera overlap, and `25 km` single-linkage Haversine clustering (`spatial_cluster`) forces the model to generalise to unseen cities [C, CODE `src/data/splits.py`].
2. **Q: Why is colour jitter banned in your data augmentation pipeline when almost every standard ImageNet training recipe uses it?**
   - **A:** Physically, PM2.5 particles scatter sunlight via Mie scattering, which directly reduces colour saturation, contrast, and sky darkness in the photograph [C, CODE `src/data/transforms.py`]. Randomly jittering brightness, contrast, or saturation during training would destroy the exact physical signal that maps the image to its ground-truth AQI label [C].
3. **Q: How can a tiny 5-input `GPS + Month Metadata MLP` (`40.34` test MAE) match a pretrained CNN (`41.12` test MAE) without looking at a single pixel?**
   - **A:** Air pollution has strong regional and seasonal patterns, and most test stations in `PM25Vision` lie near training stations in the same cities [C]. We proved this is a pure spatial shortcut by evaluating on the `717` test images more than `25 km` from any training station (`test_gt25km`): there, the `GPS + Month Metadata MLP` collapses to `49.84` MAE and $R^2 = -0.0393$ (worse than guessing the mean), while the `Final Image-Only Baseline` maintains `39.60` MAE and $R^2 = 0.2747$ [R6.a, C].
4. **Q: Why did you switch from reporting the best validation-loss checkpoint (`best_ep`) to reporting the last epoch (`last_ep`)?**
   - **A:** In PFRML multitask runs, `val_loss` adds classification cross-entropy ($L_{\text{cls}}$) to $0.5 \times L_{\text{reg}}$; as the 6-class classifier became over-confident after epoch 6, $L_{\text{cls}}$ rose and caused `min(val_loss)` to pick under-trained checkpoints from epochs `1–6` even while regression MAE kept improving [C, CODE `scripts/analysis/diagnose_training_failures.py`]. Furthermore, on Fold 3 of `ResNet-18 Pretrained Tuned`, `min(val_loss)` picked epoch 1, distorting its CV mean (`44.05` last-epoch MAE), and final models trained on 100% of the training set have no validation split on which to pick a best epoch [C, R3.a, R8].
5. **Q: Why didn't you report $p$-values from a Wilcoxon signed-rank test in your 5-fold cross-validation tables?**
   - **A:** With $n = 5$ paired folds, even if Model A beats Model B on all `5/5` folds, the minimum possible two-sided Wilcoxon $p$-value is $2 / 2^5 = 0.0625$, which can never reach the standard $\alpha = 0.05$ threshold [C, L, R4]. Instead, we report the paired mean difference $\pm$ standard error ($\text{SE} = \text{SD}/\sqrt{5}$) and folds won out of 5 for CV, and use a `1,000`-resample station-level cluster bootstrap (`95% CI`) on the `729` test stations [C, R4, R6].
6. **Q: Why did you bootstrap by *station* (`station_id`) rather than by individual *image* on the test set?**
   - **A:** The `2,920` test images are clustered across `729` stations (`205` stations in `test_gt25km`), and errors on photos taken at the same station are correlated [R6.a, C]. Resampling individual images would pretend we have `2,920` independent observations and produce artificially narrow confidence intervals; resampling stations with replacement preserves within-station correlation [C, CODE `scripts/final_evaluation.py`].
7. **Q: In Paper 1 (PFRML), the prototype-guided multitask head improves AQI estimation. Why did it *hurt* accuracy (`R2` `48.14` vs `R1` `45.50` CV MAE; `45.04` vs `41.12` test MAE) in your project?**
   - **A:** That difference (`-2.63 +- 1.10` in CV, `-3.92 [-6.27, -1.58]` on test) is an established empirical finding on our setup, and our **hypothesis** for the mechanism is twofold: first, 6-class classification on noisy global street scenes only reaches `~41%` accuracy, so feeding prototype-weighted features $[\chi_i; \tilde{c}_i]$ injects noisy class probabilities into the regression MLP; second, even when the regression MLP reads only $\chi_i$ (`R2 (Eq. 5 Literal: reg_input=chi)`, `48.04` MAE), the cross-entropy loss $L_{\text{cls}}$ competes with regression for shared backbone capacity [C, L, R4.a, R6.b].
8. **Q: What was the ambiguity between Equation 5 and Figure 7 in the PFRML paper, and how did you resolve it?**
   - **A:** PFRML `Sec. II-D Eq. (5)` writes the regression prediction as $\hat{y}_i = h(\chi_i)$ on the pooled feature $\chi_i$ directly, whereas `Sec. 9. **Q: Is `MobileNetV3-S + MD-Neck (Ref 2)` one of the models proposed in the MDNet paper?**
   - **A:** No—and we explicitly document that in the code and report [C, CODE `src/models/mdnet.py`]. Paper 2's `MDNet-T` is trained from scratch (`MDNet-T Regression Scratch (60 ep)`), which suffered from lack of pretraining on our `8,176`-image dataset (`53.29` MAE); `MobileNetV3-S + MD-Neck (Ref 2)` (`44.92` MAE) is our own adaptation that grafts two of Paper 2's `MDBlock(128)` multiscale dilated blocks onto an ImageNet-pretrained `MobileNetV3-Small` backbone [C, R3.a, CODE `src/models/mdnet.py`].

When we trained `MDNet-T` from scratch with a plain regression head (`MDNet-T Regression Scratch (60 ep)`), `train_loss` fell by `>95%` and reached `53.29` MAE, and when we probed `pfrml_mdnet_t` on Fold 0 at Paper 1's lower learning rate (`lr=2e-4`), it learned (`58.15` MAE, $R^2 = 0.2910$) [R3.a, R7, CODE `scripts/analysis/diagnose_training_failures.py`].
11. **Q: Why is Fold 3 `station` CV error (`~58–65` MAE for image models, `74.33` for `Constant Median Predictor`) so much worse than Fold 0 (`~34–37` MAE for pretrained models)?**
    - **A:** Our fold diagnostic (`scripts/diagnose_folds.py`) showed that even the `Constant Median Predictor` gets `74.33` MAE on Fold 3 vs `62.31–67.18` on the other folds because four $1^\circ \times 1^\circ$ geographic cells (Delhi/NCR, Saltillo/Monterrey, Chiang Rai, Pune) contain `76.86%` (`176/229`) of Fold 3's extreme `AQI > 200` tail across just 7 stations [C, L, R2.b]. Using the **Skill Score** ($1 - \text{MAE}/\text{MAE}_{\text{median}}$) normalises each fold by its median-predictor difficulty [C, R3.a].
12. **Q: If `Final Winning Fusion Model (ep=7)` gets the best test MAE (`35.77`), why do you say it does not "solve" the metadata shortcut?**

Using a pre-committed rule (`CMA-3` minimum for $\text{epoch} \ge 6$, verified by a fresh 5-fold CV run `Fusion R1 (p_drop=0.0, CMA-3 ep=7)` achieving `44.40 +- 7.30 < 46.95`), we selected `E_final = 7` before ever running the test evaluation [C, R5.a, CODE `scripts/experiments/queue_fusion_final.py`].

## 12. Glossary of Every Term Used

- **Ablation study:** Removing or adding one model component at a time (while keeping everything else fixed) to measure how much that single part helps or hurts [C].
- **Activation function (`ReLU`, `GELU`, `Sigmoid`, `Softmax`):** A simple non-linear math rule applied after a layer so a neural network can learn curved relationships; for example, `ReLU(x) = max(0, x)` turns negative numbers into `0` and keeps positive numbers unchanged, `Sigmoid` squashes any number into $(0, 1)$, and `Softmax` turns a list of class scores into probabilities that add up to `1.0` [CODE `src/models/baselines.py`, `src/models/mdnet.py`, `src/models/pooling.py`].
- **Adjacent-class accuracy (`+-1Acc`):** The fraction of images whose predicted AQI category is either the exact true bin or at most 1 bin away ($\frac{1}{n}\sum \mathbf{1}(|\hat{b}_i - b_i| \le 1)$), e.g., predicting `Moderate` (`bin 1`) when the truth is `Good` (`bin 0`) counts as correct [CODE `src/metrics.py`].
- **Air Quality Index (AQI) / PM2.5:** A continuous pollution scale (`0` to `500+`) based on fine particulate matter ($\le 2.5\,\mu\text{m}$ diameter), grouped by the US EPA into 6 health bins (`0–50`, `51–100`, `101–150`, `151–200`, `201–300`, `>300`) [CODE `src/data/cleaning.py`].
- **Attention pooling (`Toggle T1`):** Computing a learned weighted average over the `196` spatial image patches (using softmax or sigmoid weights) so the model can focus more on sky/haze patches than foreground pavement, instead of averaging all `196` patches equally [CODE `src/models/pooling.py`].
- **Auxiliary vision-only head:** A second regression MLP attached strictly to the image feature $\chi_i$ during fusion training (with loss weight `0.5` and zero gradient to the metadata branch) to force the image backbone to keep learning visual cues [CODE `src/models/fusion.py`, `src/pfrml_losses.py`].
- **Backbone:** The main feature-extracting convolutional network (such as `MobileNetV3-Small`, `ResNet-18`, or `MDNet-T`) that turns an input `224x224` RGB image into compact numerical feature maps [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`].
- **Centered Moving Average (`CMA-3`):** Smoothing a noisy per-epoch curve by averaging each epoch's validation MAE with the epoch immediately before and after it ($\text{CMA-3}_t = (\text{MAE}_{t-1} + \text{MAE}_t + \text{MAE}_{t+1})/3$) so a single lucky epoch spike is not picked [CODE `scripts/analysis/select_fusion_epochs.py`].
- **Classification:** Predicting which discrete category (out of the 6 US EPA AQI bins) an image belongs to [CODE `src/metrics.py`].
- **Class weights (`Toggle T3`):** Multiplying each class's cross-entropy loss by $w_k \propto N_k^{-\gamma}$ ($\gamma = 0.5$, inverse square root of training class count $N_k$) so rare high-pollution images carry more weight per sample than common clean-air images [CODE `src/losses.py`].
- **Coefficient of determination ($R^2$):** A score measuring how much variance in true AQI the model explains compared to simply guessing the mean ($R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}$); `1.0` is perfect, `0.0` matches guessing the constant mean, and `< 0` is worse than guessing the mean [CODE `src/metrics.py`].
- **Confidence interval (95% CI):** A range `[low, high]` around an estimated number (such as test MAE or the MAE difference between two models) that captures the true value 95% of the time under repeated station sampling; if a paired difference CI does not include `0`, the difference is statistically significant [C, R6].
- **Convolutional Neural Network (CNN):** An image network that slides small learnable pixel filters (like `3x3` grids) across a photo to detect edges, textures, and haze [CODE `src/models/baselines.py`].
- **Cosine annealing & linear warmup:** A learning-rate schedule that gently increases step size from near zero over the first `3` epochs (warmup) and then smoothly decreases it following a cosine curve to `1e-6` at the final epoch [CODE `train.py`, `configs/default.yaml`].
- **Cross-entropy loss ($L_{\text{cls}}$):** The standard classification loss function that measures how far predicted class probabilities are from the true class label (with **label smoothing** $\epsilon = 0.1$ softening the target from `[0, 0, 1, 0, 0, 0]` toward uniform so the classifier does not become overly confident) [CODE `src/losses.py`].
- **Cross-validation (5-fold CV):** Partitioning the training set into 5 disjoint subsets ("folds") and training 5 separate models—each using 4 folds for training and 1 fold for validation—to get a reliable mean $\pm$ SD performance estimate [CODE `src/data/splits.py`].
- **Dark Channel Prior (DCP) & atmospheric transmission ($t$):** An atmospheric optics rule stating that in haze-free outdoor patches (`15x15` pixels), at least one RGB colour channel is very dark (near `0`), whereas haze raises that minimum brightness and lowers estimated light transmission $t = 1 - \omega D(x)$ and increases extinction $-\ln(t)$ [CODE `src/features/haze_features.py`].
- **Data leakage:** Any flaw where information from the validation or test set leaks into training (such as duplicate images across splits, random splits sharing cameras, or tuning hyperparameters on the test set), producing fake accuracy [C, CODE `src/data/cleaning.py`, `src/data/splits.py`].
- **Depthwise dilated convolution (`MDBlock`):** A convolution where each channel is filtered separately (**depthwise**) and filter weights are spaced $d$ pixels apart (**dilated**, giving an effective window $k' = k + (k-1)(d-1)$), allowing `MDBlock` to combine `5x5`, `3x3`, and `1x1` fields of view cheaply [CODE `src/models/mdnet.py`].
- **Dimension Consistency Module (DCM):** PFRML's neck module that projects four backbone stage feature maps (at strides `4, 8, 16, 32`) to `224` channels, resizes them all to `14x14`, and adds them element-wise (or concatenates them in `Toggle T4`) [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`].
- **Exponential Moving Average (EMA):** Updating a stored vector by keeping 90% of its previous value and adding 10% of the current batch's average ($p_k \leftarrow 0.9\,p_k + 0.1\,\bar{x}_k$), used to update PFRML's 6 class prototypes smoothly without gradients [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`].
- **Feature map:** A 3D grid of numbers `[C, H, W]` output by a stage of a vision backbone, representing what visual patterns appear where in the image [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`].
- **Haversine distance:** The exact distance in kilometres between two `(latitude, longitude)` points along the curved surface of the Earth [CODE `src/data/splits.py`].
- **Hyperparameter:** A configuration knob set before training begins (e.g., learning rate `lr=1e-3`, epochs `30`, batch size `32`), as opposed to model weights learned by gradient descent [CODE `configs/default.yaml`].
- **LayerNorm / `LayerNorm2d`:** Normalising feature values across the channel dimension at each pixel or token to have mean `0` and variance `1`, which stabilises training regardless of batch size [CODE `src/models/mdnet.py`].
- **Macro-F1 & TailF1:** The unweighted average of per-class $F_1$ scores ($F_1 = \frac{2 \cdot \text{precision} \cdot \text{recall}}{\text{precision} + \text{recall}}$) across all 6 AQI classes (`Macro-F1`) or across just the two most polluted classes (`Bins 5–6`, `AQI > 200`, `TailF1`), so rare hazardous days count equally with common clean days [CODE `src/metrics.py`].
- **Mean Absolute Error (MAE) & TailMAE:** The average absolute error in AQI units between true pollution $y_i$ and predicted pollution $\hat{y}_i$:
  $$\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} |y_i - \hat{y}_i|$$
  where `TailMAE` computes the same formula restricted to samples in the top two US EPA bins (`AQI > 200`) [CODE `src/metrics.py`].
- **Mean Squared Error (MSE):** The average squared regression error $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i/100 - \hat{y}_i/100)^2$ used as our continuous training loss $L_{\text{reg}}$ [CODE `src/losses.py`].
- **Median:** The middle value of a sorted list of numbers (used in `src/data/cleaning.py` to merge conflicting multi-station AQI labels and in `Constant Median Predictor` as our baseline prediction) [CODE `src/data/cleaning.py`, `src/models/baselines.py`].
- **Metadata & `Metadata Zeroed at Eval` (`_meta_off`):** Non-visual sample attributes—here the 5 numbers $[x, y, z, \sin(\text{month}), \cos(\text{month})]$; in `_meta_off` evaluation, the `32`-D metadata embedding $m$ is replaced by all zeros at test time to measure how well a fusion model works from the image alone [CODE `src/data/dataset.py`, `src/models/fusion.py`].
- **Mie scattering:** The physical scattering of light by airborne particles of similar size to visible wavelengths (like PM2.5), which reduces contrast and washes out colour saturation [C, CODE `src/data/transforms.py`].
- **Modality dropout (`p_drop`):** Randomly setting a sample's `32`-D metadata embedding $m$ to all zeros with probability $p_{\text{drop}}$ (e.g., `0.3`) during training so a fusion model cannot rely 100% on location [CODE `src/models/fusion.py`].
- **Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP):** A simple feedforward neural network made of linear matrix-multiply layers and activations (e.g., `5 -> 64 -> 32 -> 1` in `MetadataMLP`) [CODE `src/models/baselines.py`].
- **Multimodal fusion:** A model that combines two different types of input data ("modalities")—here, street-level image features $\chi_i$ and GPS/month metadata features $m$—to make a single prediction [CODE `src/models/fusion.py`].
- **Multitask learning / hybrid loss:** Training one shared network to perform classification and regression simultaneously using $L = L_{\text{cls}} + \beta L_{\text{reg}}$ ($\beta = 0.5$) [CODE `src/losses.py`].
- **Overfitting:** When a model memorises noise or station-specific details in the training set (driving `train_loss` near zero) so its error on unseen validation/test stations stops improving or gets worse [C].
- **Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient (PLCC):** The standard linear correlation $r \in [-1, 1]$ between true and predicted AQI values [CODE `src/metrics.py`].
- **Physics Prior Gate (`Toggle T2`):** A module that computes 4 handcrafted optical haze numbers (`[entropy, rms_contrast, transmission, extinction]`), projects them to `224` dimensions, and blends them with the deep visual feature via a learned `[0, 1]` sigmoid gate $\alpha$ [CODE `src/models/physics_gate.py`].
- **Pretraining / transfer learning vs. from-scratch:** Initializing a backbone with weights pre-learned on ImageNet (`1.28M` images) instead of random numbers ("from scratch") [C, CODE `src/models/baselines.py`].
- **Prototypes (`K=6`):** Six unit-length reference vectors in `224`-D feature space—one representing the centre of each AQI class—used to classify images by nearest distance and to form the prototype-weighted feature $\tilde{c}_i = \sum_k p_k c'_k$ [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`].
- **Receptive field (RF):** The pixel width of the square region in the input image that can influence a single feature in a deep layer (`1136` pixels for `MDNet-T`) [L, CODE `src/models/mdnet.py`].
- **Regression:** Predicting a continuous numeric output (such as an exact AQI value of `84.2`) [CODE `src/metrics.py`].
- **Residual connection (`x + y`):** Adding a block's input `x` directly to its output `y` so gradients flow easily through deep networks without vanishing [CODE `src/models/mdnet.py`].
- **Ridge regression:** Linear regression with an $L_2$ weight penalty ($\alpha \sum w_j^2$) chosen by internal cross-validation (`RidgeCV`) to prevent overfitting on correlated features [CODE `src/models/baselines.py`].
- **Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE):** $\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}$, an error metric in AQI units that penalises large errors more heavily than MAE [CODE `src/metrics.py`].
- **Shortcut / spurious correlation:** An easy non-causal cue in the dataset (like GPS location in cities with many stations) that lets a model get low test error without learning the physical visual task [C].
- **Single-linkage clustering (`spatial_cluster`):** Grouping stations into city clusters by linking any two stations within `25.0 km` of each other into the same connected component [C, CODE `src/data/splits.py`].
- **Skill score:** The fractional reduction in MAE relative to the trivial `Constant Median Predictor`:
  $$\text{Skill} = 1 - \frac{\text{MAE}}{\text{MAE}_{\text{median}}}$$
  where `0.0` means no better than guessing the training median, `+0.31` means 31% lower error than the median baseline, and `< 0` means worse than the median baseline [C, R3, R4, R5].
- **Spearman Rank Correlation Coefficient (SRCC):** Correlation $\rho \in [-1, 1]$ computed on the *ranks* of predictions and true values, measuring whether the model orders clean-to-polluted days correctly even if the scale is non-linear [CODE `src/metrics.py`].
- **Station-level cluster bootstrap:** A statistical resampling method for test confidence intervals where we randomly draw $G$ monitoring stations *with replacement* (`1,000` times, `seed=42`)—bringing along all photos belonging to each drawn station—and take the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resulting 1,000 MAEs (or paired MAE differences) so that multiple photos from the same station are never treated as independent [C, R6, CODE `scripts/final_evaluation.py`, `scripts/analysis/compare_final_models.py`].
- **StratifiedGroupKFold:** A cross-validation splitter that keeps group IDs (`station_id` or `spatial_cluster`) strictly separated across folds while keeping the 6 AQI class proportions as balanced as possible [CODE `src/data/splits.py`].
- **Transformer encoder:** A sequence-modelling block that uses multi-head self-attention across all `196` (`14x14`) spatial image tokens (plus a `[CLS]` token) to relate distant regions of an image, such as the sky and the horizon [CODE `src/models/dcm.py`].
- **Wilcoxon signed-rank test:** A non-parametric paired statistical test across folds whose minimum possible two-sided $p$-value at $n=5$ folds is $0.0625$ [C, CODE `src/metrics.py`].
